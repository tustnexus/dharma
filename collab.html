Padma Shri Recipients: Minority & Tribal Languages

  • Naresh Chandra Dev Varma (2026): Recognized for his contribution to Kokborok literature and education. [MHA Official List]
  • Shafi Shauq (2026): Honored for his contributions to Kashmiri literature and linguistics. [Hindustan Times]
  • Charan Hembram (2026): Awarded for his efforts in promoting the Santali language and the Ol Chiki script.
  • Mahendra Kumar Mishra (2026): Honored for documenting the myths and folklore of Odisha tribal languages.
  • Pokhila Lekthepi (2026): Awarded for her work in preserving the Karbi language and folk traditions. [PIB India]
  • Anil Kumar Boro (2025): Recognized for his pioneering work in developing the Bodo language and culture.
  • Silbi Passah (2024): Recognized for her efforts in promoting the Khasi language and culture. [Padma Awards Citation]
  • Janum Singh Soy (2023): A scholar from Jharkhand who spent over 40 years preserving the Ho language.
  • Dhaniram Toto (2023): Recognized for preserving the endangered Toto language in West Bengal.
  • B. Ramakrishna Reddy (2023): Awarded for the conservation of endangered Dravidian languages like Kuvi, Manda, and Kui.
  • Ramachandraiah (2022): An oral historian from Telangana preserving the Koya language traditions. [Times of India]
  • Dhaneswar Engti (2022): An author and poet dedicated to the Karbi language of Assam.
  • T. Senka (2022): Author from Nagaland who has written books to preserve the endangered Ao language.
  • Akhone Asgar Ali Basharat (2022): A poet and author from Ladakh who works to popularize the Balti language.
  • Vidyanand Sarek (2022): Recognized for his work in Sirmauri, a language from Himachal Pradesh.
  • Narasingha Prasad Guru (2022): Known for developing a dictionary for Koshali in Odisha.
  • Mangal Singh Hazowary (2021): Awarded for his extensive literary work in the Bodo language. [PIB India]
  • Damayanti Beshra (2020): A Santali author and researcher honored for her contributions to Santali literature.
  • Zakia (2018): Honored for sustaining the endangered Mara language in Mizoram.
  • G.N. Devy (2014): Renowned activist who led the survey documenting 780 living Indian languages. [Wikipedia]
  • Anvita Abbi (2013): Recognized for research on tribal languages of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
